## Late session — May 2026 user activity: do we even track logins? → yes, better

Started as a simple question ("do we record login date/time in Metabase?") and turned into a usage-analytics setup.

**What the data layer actually has** (verified in treasury source, not just schema):
- `users` (Devise `:trackable`): `current/last_sign_in_at` + IPs + `sign_in_count` + `last_seen_at` — but overwritten per login, no history, and deliberately excluded from `audited` (treasury `app/models/user.rb:59`).
- **`user_activity_logs` is the real asset** — `Users::TrackActivityLogs` writes a row on every session start (>30 min idle gap) or IP/UA change: timestamp, IP, user-agent. Admin impersonations logged separately via `impersonation_info` jsonb → filterable. Prod/demo only.
- Caveat learned: timestamps are UTC — shift to `America/Caracas` before any hour-of-day grouping or peak-usage reads are off by 4.
- Structural note: `user_activity_logs` has no `workspace_id`; activity is per *user*, workspaces attach via `workspace_users` (many-to-many). "Activity across workspaces" = per-user cut with workspaces aggregated.

**Shipped:** [[../data/may-2026-user-activity]] — 3 Metabase-ready queries:
1. **Q1 per-user May summary** — segments (power ≥16 active days / regular / light / churned-in-may), `dropping_off` flag (engaged weeks 1–2, silent weeks 4–5), weekly trajectory w1–w5, April→May delta, days-since-seen, typical hour. Tesote staff flagged `internal`, not excluded.
2. **Q2 workspace rollup** — seats vs active vs engaged (≥10 days), `pct_seats_active`; near-0% workspaces = account-level churn risk.
