# Content + Customer Marketer — Assessment Plan

## TL;DR

Hiring **Vero's #2** — the writer-operator of an AI-augmented 2-person marketing team that runs 10 people's worth of output via Claude Code skills. **Vero** (current marketing lead, PMM/ops-leaning, head of marketing de facto) owns strategy, rubrics, and editorial review. **#2** owns craft work, skill operation, and the pillars Vero doesn't naturally own: Customer Advocacy, Founder-Led Content, Lifecycle & Expansion.

This doc defines (1) the role framing, (2) the skillset rubric, (3) the 3-stage assessment with exact prompts and scoring rubrics.

**Owners:** Luis + Vero (joint decision-makers on the hire).

## Role framing

Vero is the current marketing lead — head of marketing de facto (formal title is narrower; worth a scope/title conversation post-hire). She's a trusted operator with deep product + sales knowledge, PMM/ops-leaning. She carries **Product Marketing Discipline** (Tier 1 #2) well — product + sales intuition formalized — and owns strategy, positioning, rubrics, and editorial review across everything marketing ships.

Where Vero does NOT naturally run: the writing-heavy, storytelling-heavy pillars. That's what #2 is for.

So #2 owns:

- **Customer Advocacy Engine** (Tier 1 #1) — customer stories, reference program, reviews
- **Founder-Led Content** (Tier 1 #3) — LinkedIn ghostwriting, bottom-funnel SEO
- **Lifecycle & Expansion** (Tier 1 #4) — onboarding, nurture, customer newsletter

Operating model (full treatment in [[roles-in-ai-augmented-team]]): each pillar becomes one or more Claude Code skills (`/customer-story`, `/linkedin-week`, `/seo-piece`, `/onboarding-series`, `/customer-newsletter`).

- **Vero owns:** strategy, positioning, rubrics, editorial review, cross-functional alignment
- **#2 owns:** craft work skills can't do (interviews, ghostwriting, final polish), running the skills for their three pillars, feeding rubric-drift signals back to Vero
- **Skills own:** structure, consistency, throughput, cross-system aggregation, trigger firing

**Critical for hiring:** the candidate is *not* joining to own marketing strategy. They're joining to be Vero's writer-operator complement and to run the skill system for their three pillars. "Wants to own strategy" is a red flag for this specific seat.

## What "top 0.01% leveraging Claude Code" looks like for this seat

Ranked. If they fail #1 or #2, it's a no regardless of the rest.

1. **Writing taste in EN + ES, LatAm B2B fintech register.** Can tell good copy from slop in 5 seconds. The human-in-the-loop craft (customer interviews, founder ghostwriting, final polish) is the core of the role. Non-negotiable.
2. **Framework-design instinct — as a collaborator, not the owner.** Looks at a messy workflow and can produce a rubric (inputs, outputs, quality bar). In the real job, Vero owns the final rubric; #2 needs this instinct to (a) work skillfully within Vero's rubrics, (b) notice when rubrics are drifting, (c) design their own sub-workflows. Skills without rubrics are slop generators, so the capability matters even though the ownership doesn't.
3. **Model fluency.** Treats Claude as a collaborator — pushes back, iterates, knows when output is 70% vs 95%. Doesn't accept first drafts.
4. **Operator instincts.** Comfortable triggering skills, running cadences, wiring HubSpot + Fireflies + Intercom + Linear into a workflow, shipping on schedule. Writer-first with operator instincts — not pure craft, not pure ops.
5. **Customer curiosity.** Genuinely enjoys getting a Venezuelan CFO on a call and pulling the story out. This is the interview craft no skill can replace.

Explicit **non-requirements:** traditional marketing pedigree, brand agency experience, demand-gen background, MBA, strategic/positioning ownership ambitions (that seat is Vero's).

## The 3-stage assessment

Total candidate time: ~7 hours. Total Luis time: ~75 min before the trial. Total cost: $2–3k (Stage 3 only).

**Compressed 2026-05-04** — old Stages 1 and 2 collapsed into a single in-person design session run by Luis solo, plus a tighter async pre-screen filter and a parallel Vero touchpoint. Old format preserved under "Superseded" below for the record. Vero alignment on the new format pending.

### Stage 1 — Async pre-screen (~60 min, take-home)

Filters bilingual-fail before they hit Luis's calendar. Sent before any in-person time is committed.

**What you send:**

- 1 Fireflies transcript of a real Tesote customer call (30-min call, light redaction)
- The Tesote voice guide
- 24-hour turnaround

**The prompt:**

> Draft a LinkedIn post in Luis Pulgar's voice from this customer transcript — ~150 words, EN and ES versions both. Send back within 24 hours.

**Read = binary "would Luis post this?"** No scoring rubric beyond that.

**Auto-fail triggers:**

- ES post is obvious EN→ES machine translation (literal, stiff, pan-LatAm neutral)
- LinkedIn reads like generic B2B slop ("leveraged," "streamlined," "unlocked value")
- No specifics from the transcript — just adjectives
- Late delivery without explanation

Pass = invite to Stage 2.

### Stage 2 — In-person design session (45 min, Luis-only)

The job in miniature: design a system, not build one. Tests creative thinking, tool fluency, and content structuring under time pressure. In-person so Luis can read culture directly — energy, pushback handling, hands-dirty appetite.

**Pre-context:** candidate has already read the voice guide and one transcript from Stage 1. No new pre-work.

**Open verbatim (~3 min):**

> The goal of this hour is to see how you think through a real problem we have. I'll give you the tools at your disposal, the data points we already collect, and the outcome we want. Your job: design the skill that turns one into the other — what gets used, what gets ignored, what shape the output takes, what humans do, what the system does, and how this runs on a 2-week cadence without me babysitting it.
>
> No code. No drafts. We're designing the system. I'll push back. Use the whiteboard.

**Then declare three things (~2 min):**

1. **Tools at your disposal** — Claude Code + skills, HubSpot (CRM), Fireflies (call transcripts), Intercom (support), Linear (product), Tesote site CMS, Notion, LinkedIn. Vero (editorial review). The candidate themselves for craft work. *List the surfaces, not every API call.*
2. **Data points already collected** — won/closed deals + ARR + segment in HubSpot; full call transcripts in Fireflies; support thread history in Intercom; product usage signals (volume processed, accounts active) from internal DB.
3. **Outcome wanted** — a Tesote customer case study, fortnightly cadence, ready to ship to the marketing site + a LinkedIn post + a sales-enablement snippet. Bilingual EN/ES. Quality bar: a CFO would forward it to a peer.

**The exercise (~30 min):** candidate works on the whiteboard. Luis pushes back at least twice during the design.

**Probes (~10 min) — pick 3-4, not all:**

- "Where does this skill fail first?"
- "What's the worst case study this could ship, and how do you catch it?"
- "Vero kills your draft Friday afternoon. What's your move?"
- "What would v0.1 look like — what do you cut to ship next week?"
- "Customer interview you still remember from years ago — tell me about it." *(only if you want the reverence read)*

**What you're reading during the 30 min:**

| Signal | What "good" looks like |
|---|---|
| Asks before designing | "Who picks the customer? Who runs the interview? What kills a draft?" — these are the right questions. Jumping straight to architecture without asking = flag. |
| Human/system separation | Automating the customer interview itself = fail. Leaving everything to humans = fail. The role is the seam. |
| Trigger + rubric, not just output | Fortnightly cadence is the test — what fires it, what feeds it, what gates it before ship. Most candidates design the artifact and forget the system around it. |
| Bilingual handling | Translate-after vs. draft-native is a real call. They should have a view. |
| Failure-mode design | What happens when the customer story is thin? When the metric isn't shareable? When Vero kills it 3 cycles in a row? |

**Auto-fail triggers:**

- Designs a system where Claude does the customer interview itself
- Wants to own strategy / positioning (asks about Q3 launch priorities, not customer stories)
- Bristles or goes flat when Luis pushes back
- Cannot articulate what the skill should never do
- No questions for Luis at the end

**Vero parallel read (30 min, within 48 hrs of Stage 2):**

Separate sync — Vero meets the candidate solo. She reads working-relationship + house-style fit. Her veto stays intact: if Vero says no, it's no.

Brief Vero before her sync:

- "I read system design + culture. You read working-relationship + house-style fit. We compare notes after."
- Standard line for her to deliver verbatim: *"Just so you know how this role works — I edit your stuff hard and fast. How does that land?"*

**Decision after Stage 2 + Vero sync:** advance to Stage 3 paid trial or pass. Both Luis and Vero must vote yes; either no kills it.

### Stage 3 — 1-week paid trial ($2–3k)

Only run with the top 1–2 candidates after Stages 1–2.

**Why `/customer-story` as the Stage 3 subject** (previously open — resolved): it's the highest-leverage Tier 1 skill, it exercises the interview craft (the single least-automatable thing in the role), and shipping one well is bet-worthy evidence. `/linkedin-week` is faster to evaluate but lower-stakes; Stage 2 already tests that workflow under time pressure.

**The deliverable:**

Ship `/customer-story` v0.1 end-to-end against a real Tesote customer.

- Interview the customer (Luis intros)
- Produce: case study, logo permission, quote library, 3 social-ready snippets (EN + ES)
- Commit the skill itself to the marketing skills repo
- Document the framework, quality rubric, and a short retrospective ("what I'd change in v0.2")

**What you score:**

- Did they ship? (binary — no ship, no hire)
- Is the skill usable by others, or bespoke to this one run?
- Story quality — would you send it to a prospect?
- Customer experience during interview — would the customer agree to a second round?
- Retrospective — do they see their own gaps?

No rubric here beyond: *"Would I bet the next year of marketing on this person?"*

## Decision framework

- **Auto-hire:** Stage 1 passes filter, Stage 2 reads as system thinker + culture fit, Vero votes yes, Stage 3 ships cleanly and reusably.
- **Auto-reject:** Any auto-fail trigger in Stage 1 or 2, or Vero veto.
- **Discuss:** middle cases. Weight Stage 2 heaviest — it's the job in miniature.

**Decision-makers:** Luis + Vero jointly. Vero has veto — she's the one who'll work with this person daily, and the role is defined as her complement. If Vero says no, it's no.

## Red flags / green flags

**Red flags:**

- Treats Claude as autocomplete, not collaborator
- "I'd hire an agency for the video" — we are not building an agency stack
- No opinion on competitors or market positioning *(having opinions is fine — wanting to own strategy is the red flag; see next bullet)*
- **Wants to own marketing strategy / positioning** — that seat is Vero's; this one is writer-operator complement
- Bristles at the idea of working under a strategic lead / being heavily edited
- Can't articulate what "bad AI output" looks like
- LinkedIn posts that could be written by any B2B marketer anywhere, for any company
- Asks zero questions about Tesote customers

**Green flags:**

- Has shipped a Claude Code skill (or similar agent/workflow) without being asked to
- Shows taste in rejection — "this is bad because X"
- Asks about our HubSpot / Fireflies / Intercom stack unprompted
- Speaks about customers like individual humans, not segments
- Has a point of view about LatAm B2B fintech voice
- Brings a writing sample that makes you want to forward it
- **Excited by the "Vero owns strategy, I own craft + skill ops" split** — reads it as clean division of labor and a chance to do the best writing of their career, not as a demotion
