# Roles in an AI-Augmented Marketing Team

*Companion to [[function-map-ai-roadmap]]. That doc says **what** we do; this doc says **who does it** once skills are doing the mechanical work.*

> **Promoted to Notion 2026-04-22** as a child of the Function Map page. Notion is the source of truth going forward — edit there, re-pull here.

## The reframe

The function map reads org-level — pillars, tiers, #1 vs #2 shape. That's useful but it skips the real question: **Vero is already the marketing lead.** She sets direction, strategy, angle, framing, positioning. So the question is not "which pillar goes to whom" — it's:

1. What does Vero's day-to-day actually look like in a world where `/customer-story`, `/product-launch`, `/linkedin-week`, etc. exist and work?
2. What role does #2 play inside that system — not as "second marketer" but as a specific complement to Vero + the skills?
3. Where are the humans load-bearing, and where are they just redundant?

The answer, in one line: **Vero becomes the editor-in-chief of the skill system; #2 becomes the writer-operator who runs it and does the craft work skills can't.** Skills own structure, throughput, and consistency. Humans own judgment, relationships, and taste.

---

## The workflow spine

Every marketing asset — a case study, a launch email, a LinkedIn post, an SEO piece, a newsletter — follows the same 6-step spine:

```
Strategy  →  Rubric  →  Input  →  Draft  →  Review  →  Ship
```

| Step | What it is | Owned by (today) | Owned by (AI-augmented) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Strategy** | Why this, why now, what angle | Vero | Vero |
| **Rubric** | What "good" looks like for this asset | (implicit) | Vero (explicit, versioned) |
| **Input** | Raw material (customer call, deal history, product release) | Vero | Skill pulls from systems; #2 fills gaps |
| **Draft** | First shape of the asset | Vero | Skill |
| **Review** | Editorial judgment, polish, kill-or-ship | Vero | Vero (sometimes #2) |
| **Ship** | Publish, distribute, measure | Vero | #2 |

Old world: Vero does all six, slowly, inconsistently, and becomes the bottleneck by month 3.
New world: Vero does Strategy + Rubric + Review at ~10× throughput. Skills do Draft. #2 does Input gathering, Ship, and the craft work no skill can do.

## Vero's role: editor-in-chief + strategist

### What Vero owns (and only Vero can do)

**1. Strategy & positioning — the taste layer**
- ICP definition, messaging framework, category POV
- Competitive stance — what we claim, what we won't claim, what we concede
- Launch cadence and priority calls — what ships with a big push vs. a quiet one
- Pricing & packaging direction (in lockstep with Luis + sales)
- The **house style** — what Tesote sounds like, looks like, feels like

Without this layer, the skills produce generic fintech marketing. Vero is what makes the output *Tesote's*.

**2. Rubric design — make "good" explicit**

This is the single highest-leverage thing Vero can invest in. For every skill, there's a quality rubric:

- `/customer-story` → what makes a Tesote case study land? (specific metric, named buyer pain, credible dollar figure, quote with teeth)
- `/battlecard` → what framing wins vs. Competitor X? (where we punch, where we dodge)
- `/linkedin-week` → what does a Luis post sound like? (opinionated, specific, mined from a real call, no platitudes)
- `/product-launch` → what does a Tesote launch look like? (customer-proof in the first 3 lines, not features)

Rubrics are **versioned documents**, not tribal knowledge. They're what make the skills consistent across time, across operators, and across the human/AI seam. If Vero holds rubrics only in her head, the skills stay mediocre and she stays the bottleneck.

**3. Input sourcing — the relationships**
- Lives in sales calls, win/loss reviews, customer conversations
- Has the trust with sales/CS/product to pull raw material the skills need
- Decides *which* customer's story is worth running through `/customer-story` next
- Decides which competitor deserves a refreshed battlecard this quarter

Skills can pull structured data (HubSpot, Fireflies, Intercom). They can't walk into a sales leader's Slack DM and ask "who should we interview?"

**4. Editorial review — final pass before ship**
- AI drafts → Vero reviews → ship
- She's not writing from scratch; she's the editor. That's a different cognitive load and ~5× faster.
- She kills drafts that miss the rubric, instead of polishing mediocre ones.

**5. Cross-functional alignment**
- Marketing serves sales → she's the bridge to Nelson / sales leadership
- Founder content direction → she's Luis's thought partner on angle and timing
- Launch coordination → she's the bridge to product

### What Vero stops doing

- Writing first drafts from scratch
- Manually pulling customer context across 4 systems before starting an asset
- Ghostwriting LinkedIn from a blank page
- Formatting, packaging, distributing
- Remembering which customer agreed to be a reference last March

### Vero's time split (target, once skills are live)

- 40% strategy, positioning, rubric work
- 30% review + judgment calls on AI drafts
- 20% input sourcing (customer/sales/product conversations)
- 10% coordination with Luis, #2, sales, product

## #2's role: writer-operator + craft worker

#2 is not "Vero's assistant" and not "the other marketer." #2 is specifically **the writer-operator who runs the skill system and does the human-craft work skills can't replace**.

Per `people/vero.md`, Vero is PMM/ops-leaning, so #2 should be content/customer-leaning — the complement. This role description follows from that.

### What #2 owns

**1. Craft work no skill can do**
- **Customer interviews** — building rapport on a call, hearing the real story behind the metric, catching the quote that wasn't in the HubSpot note. This is a writer's job, not a prompt's.
- **Founder ghostwriting** — sitting with Luis, mining a call, hearing what he actually means, putting it in his voice. `/linkedin-week` gets you 80% there; the last 20% is a writer with an ear.
- **Final human polish** — the paragraph that needs to be cut from 4 sentences to 2. The subject line that needs to stop being clever.

**2. Runs the pillars Vero doesn't naturally own**
- **Customer Advocacy** — operator of `/customer-story`, `/reference-match`, `/review-nudge`
- **Founder-Led Content** — operator of `/linkedin-week`, `/seo-piece`
- **Lifecycle & Expansion** — operator of `/onboarding-series`, `/customer-newsletter`, `/expansion-signal`

"Operator" means: triggers the skill, feeds it the right inputs, reviews the draft against Vero's rubric, does the human polish, ships.

**3. Feeds intel back up to Vero**
- "This LinkedIn angle is landing; the other one's flat — we should adjust the voice guide"
- "Customer X's story has a messaging line we should steal for positioning"
- "The `/battlecard` output against Competitor Y is getting generic — the rubric might need an update"

#2 is the feedback loop that keeps the rubric system honest. They operate it enough to notice when it's drifting.

**4. Ship & distribution operations**
- Publishing cadences, LinkedIn scheduling, newsletter sends
- Landing page updates tied to campaigns
- Review site / advocacy logistics

### What #2 does NOT own

- Strategy, positioning, pricing, competitive stance — all Vero
- Rubric-setting — all Vero (#2 suggests updates, Vero decides)
- Product Marketing Discipline (launches, enablement, win/loss, battlecards) — all Vero
- MarTech ops — all Vero
- Paid media, ABM, events at scale — nobody (Tier 2 no-go)

### Why this shape

The risk of hiring another strategist: you get two people with opinions and nobody writing. The risk of hiring a pure writer with no operator instinct: the skill system doesn't run — skills need a human to trigger, feed, and ship them. So #2 is **writer-first with operator instincts** — comfortable running workflows, triggering skills, reading dashboards, but the core talent is craft.

## What the skills own

The skills are the **multiplier layer** between Vero's judgment and the world.

- **Structure** — every case study, battlecard, launch has the same shape. No reinventing.
- **Throughput** — a 2-person team can produce at 10-person volume because drafts are ~free.
- **Consistency** — the rubric is enforced every time, not "when we remember."
- **Cross-system aggregation** — skills pull from HubSpot + Fireflies + Intercom + Tesote CLI + Linear so humans don't.
- **Trigger-based firing** — new customer closes → `/customer-story` fires. Product release tagged → `/product-launch` fires. Weekly cadence → `/linkedin-week` fires. No one has to remember.

Skills do **not** own: judgment, taste, relationships, strategic calls, or anything that requires a human-in-the-room. If a skill is producing output nobody wants to ship, the fix is almost always rubric-level (Vero), not prompt-level.

## The rhythm — what a week looks like

Once this is running, here's what a normal marketing week looks like:

**Monday**
- `/linkedin-week` fires → draft 3 Luis posts
- #2 reviews drafts, interviews Luis if needed, polishes, schedules
- Vero reviews final week's slate before send

**Ongoing triggers (through the week)**
- Deal closes in HubSpot → `/customer-story` fires → #2 reaches out to customer to schedule interview
- Product release tagged → `/product-launch` fires → Vero reviews, #2 ships across channels
- Customer hits success milestone → `/review-nudge` fires → #2 personalizes and sends

**Monthly**
- 2 SEO pieces via `/seo-piece` — #2 drafts + polishes, Vero reviews
- 2 customer stories shipped — #2 conducts interviews, runs `/customer-story`, Vero edits

**Quarterly**
- `/battlecard` refresh — Vero owns, pulls from `/win-loss` outputs
- `/customer-newsletter` — #2 runs, Vero reviews
- Rubric review — Vero audits skill outputs, updates rubrics where drift is creeping in

**Always**
- Vero is in sales/customer/product conversations feeding inputs back into the system
- #2 is writing, interviewing, or polishing
- Skills are doing the mechanical middle

## What this means for the hire

The #2 hire already reframed once; this sharpens it further. The job is:

- **60% craft** — writing, interviewing, ghostwriting, polishing
- **30% operator** — running skill workflows, triggering, shipping, distributing
- **10% feedback** — spotting rubric drift and flagging it to Vero

Signals in the hiring bar:
- Has written for B2B SaaS/fintech before (not consumer, not agency)
- Has interviewed customers and turned it into a story (not just written *about* product)
- Comfortable running workflows / ops-adjacent (not "I just write, someone else ships")
- Okay being edited hard and fast (skills output a lot; Vero edits a lot)
- LatAm context or willing to learn it fast

Red flags:
- Wants to own strategy
- Thinks AI = threat, not tool
- Needs every piece to be bespoke / "artisan"
- No operator instincts — can't run a publishing calendar

## What this means for Vero

Three things to sort with her:

1. **Title / scope** — her formal title (PR & Communications Manager) doesn't match the reality (head of marketing). Worth a conversation post-#2-hire. Open thread in `people/vero.md`.
2. **Rubric investment** — the highest-leverage thing she can do before skills are built is write down rubrics. Otherwise skills inherit tribal knowledge and lose fidelity. This should be an explicit project, not a side effect.
3. **Calibration time with Luis** — as she moves from "doing marketing" to "directing marketing + skill system," her time with Luis shifts from status/review to strategic calibration. Cadence worth setting explicitly.
