# 🌳 Marketing Function Map & Prioritization — AI-Augmented Roadmap

*Last updated: 2026-04-16 · Owner: Luis*

## Purpose

This doc lays out (1) the full tree of functions a mature B2B SaaS/fintech marketing org covers, (2) how we prioritize them given Tesote's current stage, and (3) the operating model we're building toward — **an AI-augmented marketing function where each pillar is supported by repeatable frameworks, AI skills, and automated triggers**, so a 2-person team can operate like a 10-person team.

---

## The Vision: AI-Augmented Marketing

The goal is **not** to hire one person per marketing pillar. The goal is:

1. **Build frameworks, not ad-hoc execution.** For every repeatable workflow (a customer story, a product launch, a LinkedIn post series, a quarterly customer newsletter, an SEO piece), we define a consistent structure and quality bar *once*.
2. **Turn each framework into an AI skill.** Each workflow becomes a Claude Code skill — parameterized, repeatable, and ruthlessly consistent. The human provides judgment and inputs; the skill handles the mechanics.
3. **Automate schedules and triggers.** Campaigns, posts, and programs fire on defined cadences or triggers — e.g., a new customer closes → advocacy-intake skill triggers; a product release is tagged → launch-kit skill spins up; a week starts → LinkedIn content batch is drafted.
4. **Humans own strategy, judgment, and relationships.** AI owns structure, consistency, and throughput.

This is the same pattern we already use for sales (`/post-intro-call`, `/post-discovery`, `/proposal`, `/workspace-review`) — now applied to marketing.

## Prioritization Philosophy

Three principles override everything else:

1. **In B2B fintech, trust > creativity.** Buyers fear getting it wrong more than they crave something new. Every pillar should reinforce *"safe, credible, won't lose my money or data."* This is why fintech marketing looks "boring" relative to consumer — and why it works.
2. **Marketing serves sales.** In B2B, sales closes. Marketing's job is to make sales more efficient (shorter cycles, higher win rates, higher ACV) and feed pipeline. Measure by pipeline influence and sales velocity, not brand metrics.
3. **Narrow and deep beats broad and shallow.** Dominating a tight ICP and a few channels beats 15 mediocre pillars. Pick few, execute excellently, expand.

## Tiered Prioritization — Tesote-Calibrated

### The filter: pick only force multipliers

A customer story works for you for 3 years; an event works once. An SEO page compounds monthly; a paid ad stops when the spend stops. At a 2-person team size, **every hour must compound**.

### Tier 1 — What we focus on (the 4 things)

**1. Customer Advocacy Engine** *(highest-leverage unlock, almost certainly under-exploited)*
- Target: 2 customer stories / month (video + written) → 24/yr, each reused for years
- Reference program: 10–15 referenceable customers tagged by segment
- Reviews (G2, Capterra, regional): push to 50+
- Logo wall, quote library, segmented case-study index
- *Why first:* directly lifts sales win rate on every deal; compounds forever; low ops overhead

**2. Product Marketing Discipline** *(the quiet win-rate lever, usually missing at this stage)*
- Every release → launch email + sales enablement update + customer comms
- Battlecards for top 3 competitors, refreshed quarterly
- Messaging (ICP → pain → value → proof) tested against win/loss interviews
- Pricing & packaging strategy — likely leaking money here
- *Why second:* compounds on every deal sales runs; the difference between 25% and 40% win rate

**3. Founder-Led Content — LinkedIn + Bottom-Funnel SEO** *(cheapest, highest-trust acquisition)*
- Founder LinkedIn: 3 posts/week, opinionated, mined from real customer conversations
- SEO: 2 deep pieces/month on bottom-funnel queries — *"conciliación bancaria automática," "integración Odoo banco," "tesorería B2B LatAm"*
- LatAm fintech SEO is wide open
- *Why third:* compounds; fills pipeline without ad spend; LatAm B2B buyers trust founder voice over brand voice

**4. Lifecycle & Expansion** *(your 150 customers are the next ARR)*
- Onboarding series (reduces churn, accelerates time-to-value)
- Usage-triggered emails (adoption nudges, upsell cues)
- Quarterly customer newsletter tied to product roadmap
- *Why fourth:* expansion is cheaper than new-logo acquisition; automatable by a small team

### Tier 2 — Explicitly NOT doing (say no, loudly)

- **Paid ads at scale** — experiments only, cap at ~10% of budget
- **Events** — attend and speak, don't sponsor booths or run our own
- **ABM infrastructure** — wait until sales team doubles
- **PR / analyst relations** — overhead exceeds return at our ARR
- **Owned user conference / formal community** — 12+ months away
- **Brand refresh / redesign** — not needed
- **Heavy MarTech** — HubSpot + CMS + GA is enough; no CDP, no attribution platform

### The 2nd hire

Shape of the hire depends on who #1 is:

- If #1 is **content/generalist-leaning** → hire a **product marketer** (launches, enablement, competitive, pricing). Fastest sales-velocity impact.
- If #1 is **more PMM/ops-leaning** → hire a **content + customer marketer** (stories, founder ghostwriting, SEO). Fastest pipeline impact.

**Do NOT hire a demand gen / paid person yet.** The inputs (messaging, stories, content, positioning) aren't mature enough to feed them — they'd burn budget optimizing immature inputs.

## Turning This Into AI Skills — The Operating Model

For each Tier 1 priority, we identify the repeatable workflows and convert them into Claude Code skills, with automated schedules or triggers. This is how a 2-person team runs a mature marketing org.

### What makes a good AI skill candidate

- **Repeatable** — same shape, different inputs, many times per year
- **High-leverage** — output is an asset that gets reused or amplified
- **Data-rich** — inputs come from systems we already have (HubSpot, Fireflies, Intercom, Linear, Gmail, Slack, Tesote CLI)
- **Quality-bounded** — a clear rubric exists for what "good" looks like

### Skill candidates by priority

**Customer Advocacy**
- `/customer-story` — pull customer context from HubSpot + Intercom + Fireflies → draft case study + video prep questions + quote candidates
- `/reference-match` — given a prospect's profile, surface the 3 best customer references by segment/use case
- `/review-nudge` — triggered when a customer hits a success milestone → draft personalized G2/Capterra review request
- *Trigger examples:* new customer closes in HubSpot; product usage milestone hit; QBR completed

**Product Marketing**
- `/product-launch` — given a release tag → launch email + sales enablement update + in-app announcement + LinkedIn post + customer newsletter blurb
- `/battlecard` — refresh competitive battlecards from recent win/loss calls + public competitor signals
- `/win-loss` — pull closed-won/lost deal context from HubSpot + Fireflies → structured win/loss interview summary + messaging recommendations
- *Trigger examples:* product release tagged; deal closed (won or lost); quarterly cadence

**Founder Content**
- `/linkedin-week` — weekly: mine customer calls from Fireflies + Slack + recent product wins → draft 3 LinkedIn posts with founder's voice
- `/seo-piece` — given a bottom-funnel keyword → outline + draft + internal link map + meta package
- *Trigger examples:* weekly schedule (Mondays); monthly SEO batch

**Lifecycle & Expansion**
- `/onboarding-series` — maintain and regenerate onboarding email sequences from latest product state
- `/customer-newsletter` — quarterly: product updates + customer wins + roadmap → customer-ready newsletter
- `/expansion-signal` — triggered by usage patterns in Tesote CLI → draft expansion outreach for CSM/AE
- *Trigger examples:* new customer onboarding starts; quarterly schedule; usage threshold crossed

## Next Steps

1. Validate this prioritization with the team
2. For each Tier 1 priority, pick the **highest-leverage skill** to build first
3. Define the framework (inputs, outputs, quality rubric) *before* building the skill
4. Build, use, refine, then add the next one
5. Layer in automated schedules and triggers once the skills are stable

The point is not to build 12 skills in a quarter — it's to build 3 and use them relentlessly, then expand.
